**1) Biden, Xi and "strategic ambiguity" (l. 1–9)**  
Biden said he would be ready to use force to defend Taiwan. Xi Jinping wants "reunification" with Taiwan and does not exclude using force. China sees Taiwan as a province that broke away, but Taiwan sees itself as an independent country with its own constitution and elected leaders.  
"Strategic ambiguity" means the US does not say clearly if it would defend Taiwan militarily. The goal is to keep China uncertain.

**2) Geographic and strategic significance (l. 11–17)**  
Taiwan is an island about 160 km from the coast of China. It is part of the "first island chain", a line of territories that are friendly to the US, like Japan and the Philippines. If China controlled Taiwan, it could project power into the Pacific and maybe threaten US bases in Guam and Hawaii.

**3) Taiwan's history (l. 18–39)**  
China first controlled Taiwan in the 17th century. In 1895, China lost the island to Japan, and it got it back in 1945 after World War Two. Then there was a civil war between the nationalists of Chiang Kai-shek and Mao's communists. The communists won in 1949, and the nationalists (the Kuomintang) escaped to Taiwan.  
Today, China uses this history to say Taiwan is Chinese. Taiwan says it was never part of the modern Chinese state. Only 13 countries and the Vatican recognise Taiwan.

**4) Defence capabilities and tensions (l. 41–68)**  
China's army is much bigger: about 2 million active soldiers against 163,000 for Taiwan. In a war, Taiwan could only slow down the attack, try to stop a landing and fight a guerrilla war while waiting for help, maybe from the US. Officially, the US follows the "One-China" policy, but in 2022 Biden said "yes" when asked if he would defend Taiwan.  
Tensions grew in 2021, when China sent many military planes into Taiwan's air defence zone. The record was 56 planes in one day, in October 2021.

**5) Economic significance (l. 69–77)**  
Taiwan produces a large part of the world's computer chips, which we find in phones, laptops and game consoles. One company, TSMC, has more than half of the world market. If China took Taiwan, it could control one of the most important industries in the world.

**6) Are people worried? What do they want? (ending)**  
Not really. In 2021, almost two thirds (64.3%) of Taiwanese people did not think there would be a war with China. More and more people see themselves as Taiwanese, not Chinese. This suggests that most of them want to keep their own identity and stay independent from China.
